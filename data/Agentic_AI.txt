Artificial intelligence (AI) is the field of creating computer systems that perform tasks commonly associated with human abilities, such as understanding language, recognizing images, making predictions, and solving problems. Machine learning is an approach to AI in which models learn patterns from data. Large language models (LLMs) are machine-learning models that can understand and generate text, but their answers may be inaccurate and should be checked when correctness matters.

Agentic AI describes AI systems that work toward a goal by selecting and carrying out actions across multiple steps. An agent may plan, use tools such as search or databases, inspect the results, and decide what to do next. Unlike a fixed question-and-answer pipeline, an agent can adapt its actions to observations. Agents need appropriate permissions, limits, and checks because they can make poor decisions or repeat actions.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) combines an LLM with a search system over a collection of documents. The system finds passages relevant to a user's question and provides them to the LLM as context for its answer. RAG can help answer questions using private or updated information, and source references can help users verify answers. RAG does not guarantee correctness: retrieval may miss useful evidence, and the LLM may misinterpret the passages or produce unsupported claims.
